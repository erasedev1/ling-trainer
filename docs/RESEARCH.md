## 1. How a Senior shake actually works

**There is no "dice roll" that produces a target.** A shake is a turn-based cube game
between three players, and the letters accumulate *during play*:

1. **Setup.** 23 cubes: four each of red, black, green, pink and yellow, and three
   orange (a set containing four orange cubes uses only three in tournament play; two
   red cubes must carry a `U`). Mat has two sections: **Letters** and **Demands**.
   First player is chosen by each player rolling a red cube — closest to `A` starts.
2. **Player One** rolls *all* the cubes into a group called **Resources** and states a
   sentence **Pattern, Structure, or Purpose** (one of the three, not all three).
   Player One does **not** place a cube. (LT 6, LT 11)
3. **Player Two** states a **Type Demand** — the part of speech of the word to be
   formed — and places any black or green cube in Demands. (LT 9)
4. **Player Three** states a **Function Demand** — how that part of speech will
   function — and places a black or green cube in Demands. If no Function Demand is
   available for the type (Interjection), Player Three instead plays a letter or makes
   a General Demand. (LT 10)
5. **Every later turn** (one minute each) a player does exactly one of four things
   (LT 12): move a cube to **Letters**; make a **Demand** (General or additional
   Function); make a **Challenge**; or **PASS**. A player may not do two of these.
   A cube that touches the mat is played and cannot be moved or retracted.
6. The shake ends on a Challenge or a Forceout.

**Object of the game (LT 2):** make a **4–10 letter word** from cubes on the mat that
satisfies every demand, used in a sentence of the designated type.

### Challenges (LT 19, LT 20)

- **Challenge Now** — "I can solve this." The challenger writes a solution using the
  cubes in Letters plus **one more cube from Resources if needed**; that one cube may
  instead be a black/green cube used to make one additional Demand (which must be
  written with the solution). Requires **≥ 3 cubes in Letters**; challenging with fewer
  is −1 and the challenge is set aside. Mover and third party may solve or be Neutral.
- **Challenge Impossible** — "no solution exists with the demands in force." The
  challenger may **not** write a solution and may not add a demand. The Mover must
  solve, using as many letters from Letters *and* Resources as needed. Third party may
  solve or go Neutral.
- **Player One may Challenge Impossible** *instead of* designating a sentence type, if
  Resources make any solution impossible (LT 25). If nobody can solve, all players
  score 0.
- A player may never challenge if they made the last move. Challenge is signalled by
  picking up the challenge block while stating the challenge.

### PASS and Forceout (LT 24)

PASS is a fourth kind of move, available only after the first three set-up moves. Each
player may PASS **once per shake**. When all three have passed, **FORCEOUT** is called:
three minutes to write a solution using **two more cubes from Resources**, neither of
which may be a Demand. Correct solution = 4 points, incorrect or none = 2.

### Timing (LT 13)

| Task | Limit |
| --- | --- |
| Roll + order cubes + state sentence designation | 1 min |
| Move a cube to Letters | 1 min |
| Make a Demand | 1 min |
| Write a solution | 3 min |
| Check an opponent's solution | 2 min |

Every limit is a one-minute sand timer **plus a spoken 10-second countdown**. Failing
(1)–(3) is −1 and loss of turn; failing (4)–(5) simply forfeits the activity. A round is
**30 minutes**, then a five-minute warning to finish the current shake. Absent for a
shake = −2 (LT 34). No time-outs of any kind are permitted.

### Scoring (Scoring Chart, rev. July 2025)

| Situation | 6 | 4 | 2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A. Challenge Now, challenger correct | Challenger | Solver | Neutral, Wrong |
| B. Challenge Now, challenger wrong but someone solves | Solver | Neutral | Challenger, Wrong |
| C. Challenge Now, nobody solves | Neutral *(see LT 26 #3)* | Challenger | Wrong |
| D. Challenge Impossible, nobody solves | Challenger | Neutral | Mover, Wrong |
| E. Challenge Impossible, someone solves | Solver | Challenger | Wrong |
| F. Forceout, all agreed | — | Agreer | Neutral, Wrong |

LT 26 #3: if a player is ≥ 4 points ahead at the five-minute warning, calls Challenge
Now, and nobody has a correct solution, any **Neutral** player gets 6 instead of 4.

### What makes a word legal (LT 22) — this is the dictionary standard

Official dictionary: **Webster's Third New International Unabridged**, and during
competition specifically the **online unabridged version at dictionary.eb.com** (LT 4).
Official grammar: **Elements of Language, 6th Course** (Holt Rinehart Winston), primary;
Prentice-Hall Grammar and Composition and The Plain English Handbook secondary.

The word to be formed:

- 4–10 letters (LT 2);
- **not** a contraction, hyphenated word, or **proper noun**; no apostrophe; no
  diacritical mark;
- **not** labelled *obsolete* — but a word labelled **archaic is allowed**;
- must be **listed with a definition** in the official dictionary;
- not profanity, vulgar, slang, substandard, obscene or offensive in usage, and not a
  word whose *definition 1* is marked offensive regardless of usage;
- not an abbreviation;
- must be **used accurately according to its dictionary definition**; a `var of`
  cross-reference is followed to the alternate spelling, which carries the same meaning;
- must be used in the way it is **normally** used — "a word cannot be called an
  adjective simply because the player wishes to use it in that manner"; the dictionary
  is the authority on whether a word may be used as the demanded part of speech;
- must not appear inside a title.

### What makes a sentence legal (LT 23)

Justifiable as reality (no "in my dream" / "in the movie" escapes); grammatically
correct including subject-verb agreement; correct spelling and capitalisation; opens
with a capital and closes with correct punctuation; internal punctuation is required
only for possessives, interjections, appositives, nouns of direct address, direct
quotes, and conjunctive adverbs; not unintelligible or cumbersome; **≤ 20 words**.

## 2. The complete Senior Division demand structure

Taken from the Senior Order of Play (June 2026) and cross-checked against LT 9, LT 10
and LT 16 in the Tournament Rules. **Senior-only items are marked ★.**

### 2.1 Sentence designation (Player One picks exactly one)

**Patterns (12 in Senior — the full set):**
`S-V`, `S-V-DO`, `S-V-IO-DO`, `S-LV-PN`, `S-LV-PA`, `Inverted`,
`S-V-DO-OC (n)`, `S-V-DO-OC (adj)`,
★`S-V-Retained DO`, ★`S-V-Retained IO`, ★`S-V-Retained OC (noun)`,
★`S-V-Retained OC (adj)`.

The four Retained patterns are **Senior-exclusive** and all four **require the passive
voice** (Handbook II.A note: "Retained objects can only be found in sentences written in
the passive voice").

**Structures (same in all divisions):** simple, compound, complex, compound-complex.
**Purposes (same in all divisions):** declarative, interrogative, imperative, exclamatory.

Interaction rules that matter for training:
- The **independent clause dictates the pattern**; dependent clauses and modifiers do not.
- Pattern designated + compound sentence written → the word must be in the designated
  pattern's half.
- Pattern designated + complex sentence → the word may be in *either* clause.
- Pattern designated + compound-complex → the word may be in the designated pattern or
  in a dependent clause **attached to it**, not one attached to the other independent clause.
- Purpose designated + multi-clause sentence → satisfied if **any** independent clause
  meets the purpose, and the word need not be in that clause.
- A direct or indirect quote containing a subject and verb **is a dependent noun clause**,
  so it turns a simple sentence into a complex one.
- For this game an interrogative sentence is **never** counted as inverted.

### 2.2 Type Demands (Player Two) — 8, all available in Senior

Noun, Pronoun, Verb, Adjective, Adverb, Preposition, Conjunction, Interjection.

### 2.3 Function Demands (Player Three, and any later turn)

| Type | Senior functions |
| --- | --- |
| Noun | subject, direct object, indirect object, predicate noun, object of the preposition, appositive, noun used as adjective, objective complement, ★retained DO, ★retained IO, ★retained OC |
| Pronoun | subject, direct object, indirect object, predicate noun, object of the preposition, appositive, objective complement, ★retained DO, ★retained IO, ★retained OC |
| Verb | main verb, auxiliary, infinitive, gerund, participle |
| Adjective | noun modifier, pronoun modifier, predicate adjective, adjacent adjective, objective complement, ★retained OC |
| Adverb | verb modifier, adjective modifier, adverb modifier |
| Preposition | introductory word in an adjective phrase, introductory word in an adverb phrase |
| Conjunction | subordinator, conjunctive adverb |
| Interjection | *none* — Player Three plays a letter or makes a General Demand |

### 2.4 General Demands — Senior list (LT 16)

**A–G, all divisions, each usable once per shake** (repeating one is a Duplicate Demand,
−1):

- **A. Color wild** — one colour is wild; wild cubes may each stand for a different
  letter. Only one colour may be wild per shake.
- **B. Must contain letter** — one letter only, per shake.
- **C. Must not contain letter** — one letter only, per shake.
- **D. Letter transfer** — "all P's are X's"; P is then eliminated from the shake,
  including a wild cube designated P. One transfer per shake.
- **E. Number of letters** — exact, 4–10.
- **F. Double vowel** — two consecutive *identical* vowels (`ee`, `oo`, `aa`).
- **G. Double consonant** — two consecutive *identical* consonants (`tt`, `pp`).

**H–R, tied to the Type Demand:**

- **H. Noun** — singular\*, plural\*, collective\*\*, nominative case\*, objective case\*
  (\* not applicable to *noun used as adjective*; \*\* a collective noun used as an
  adjective cannot be singular/plural or have case).
- **I. Pronoun** — singular, plural, indefinite, personal, interrogative,
  ★demonstrative, ★relative, nominative case, objective case, possessive case.
- **J. Verb** — singular form, plural form, linking, regular, irregular, present
  participle, past participle, simple tense⁺, perfect tense⁺, progressive form⁺,
  perfect progressive form⁺, function for infinitive, function for gerund,
  ★active voice, ★passive voice. (⁺ the player may additionally specify present / past
  / future as part of the same demand, not as an extra demand.)
- **K. Adjective / L. Adverb** — positive, comparative, superlative degree; regular;
  irregular. Degree and regular/irregular may be combined in one demand or called
  separately. Modifiers with no degrees are treated as **positive**.
- **M. Clauses** (word must be *contained in*) — noun, adjective, adverb, infinitive,
  ★elliptical.
- **N. Phrases** (word must be *contained in*) — appositive, adjective, adverb,
  infinitive, gerund, participial.
  **M + N together are limited to two per shake** (2 clauses, 2 phrases, or 1 + 1).
- **O. Compound word** — one solidly written word made of two or more smaller words
  keeping their meanings, no part acting as a prefix or suffix (`goldfish`, `lipstick`).
  Note: *not* "compound preposition" (two separate words) — use a preposition that is
  itself a compound word (`into`, `upon`, `within`, `without`).
- **P. Word must be part of** — a direct quote (correct punctuation and capitalisation
  required) or an indirect quote.
- **Q. Word must NOT be contained in** — noun / adjective / adverb / infinitive /
  ★elliptical clause, direct quote, indirect quote, appositive / adjective / adverb /
  infinitive / gerund / participial phrase. **Limited to once per shake.**
- **R. ★Clause-or-phrase function** — after M or N has been demanded, a further demand
  may specify how that clause or phrase functions:
  - Noun clause: subject, DO, IO, predicate noun, obj. of prep., appositive, OC (noun)
  - Gerund phrase: subject, DO, IO, predicate noun, obj. of prep., appositive
  - Infinitive clause: DO, predicate noun, obj. of prep.
  - Infinitive phrase: subject, DO, predicate noun, obj. of prep., verb / adjective / adverb modifier
  - Adjective clause: OC (adj), noun modifier, pronoun modifier
  - Adjective phrase, participial phrase: noun modifier, pronoun modifier
  - Adverb clause, adverb phrase: verb / adjective / adverb modifier

### 2.5 What Senior adds over Junior (and over Middle/Elementary)

| Area | Senior gains |
| --- | --- |
| Patterns | the four **Retained** patterns (passive voice required) |
| Noun functions | retained DO, retained IO, retained OC |
| Pronoun functions | retained DO, retained IO, retained OC |
| Adjective functions | retained objective complement |
| Pronoun demands | **demonstrative**, **relative** |
| Verb demands | **active voice**, **passive voice** |
| Clauses | **elliptical clause** (and "must not be in an elliptical clause") |
| Demand section R | clause/phrase **function** demands (Senior only) |

Junior already has: OC functions, retained-free; cases; perfect/progressive tenses;
gerund & participial phrases; compound word; quotes; "must not be contained in".
Middle and Elementary are considerably smaller and are **not** used by this trainer.

## 3. What can and cannot be validated automatically

This is the decision that shapes the whole product.

### 3.1 Word-level — fully automatable

These constrain the **word itself**, independent of any sentence, so a generated shake
has a computable complete answer set:

- spellability from the cubes on the mat (with wild colours and letter transfer applied)
- number of letters (LT 16 E) and the 4–10 range (LT 2)
- must contain / must not contain a letter (B, C)
- double vowel / double consonant (F, G)
- compound word (O)
- **Type Demand** — is the word usable as this part of speech?
- Noun: singular / plural / collective
- Pronoun: singular / plural, indefinite / personal / interrogative / demonstrative /
  relative, and case (pronoun case *is* morphological)
- Verb: singular & plural form, linking, regular / irregular, present & past participle
- Adjective / Adverb: regular / irregular
- Verb functions that are morphological: infinitive (bare form), gerund and present
  participle (`-ing`), past participle, auxiliary (closed list)

### 3.2 Sentence-level — **not** decidable from the word alone

These are properties of the sentence the player writes, and almost any word of the right
type can satisfy them given a suitable sentence:

- every sentence **Pattern, Structure and Purpose**
- all noun/pronoun/adjective/adverb/preposition/conjunction **Function Demands**
  (subject, direct object, predicate adjective, subordinator …)
- **noun** nominative/objective case — for nouns, case is decided by function, not form
  (Handbook III.C.4-5: "in English, nouns do not have the distinctive … case form")
- clause and phrase containment (M, N, Q), quotes (P), and section R functions
- verb tense/voice demands where the auxiliary carries the tense (perfect, progressive,
  passive) — the *word to be formed* may be the participle or the auxiliary
- adjective/adverb **degree**, because `more common` makes `common` comparative
  (Handbook self-test #44) — so degree cannot be read off the word's shape

**Consequence:** a drill that types free-form words can only honestly grade §3.1. The
trainer therefore separates two kinds of content, and labels them in the UI:

- **Shake drills** — generated, machine-graded, using only §3.1 constraints, with a
  complete answer key. This is the real bottleneck skill in a live shake: *given these
  cubes and these demands, what legal words exist at all?*
- **Judgement drills** — curated items with authoritative answers, for §3.2. The
  Handbook's own Judge's Self-Test (55 questions with answers and explanations,
  Section XVIII) plus the worked examples throughout Sections II–XIII are the source.
  Nothing here is invented.

A sentence-level demand is still **displayed** on a shake drill when it is part of the
generated scenario, marked "sentence-level — not graded here", because reading a full
demand stack quickly is itself a trained skill.

### 3.3 The dictionary problem

The official dictionary is Webster's Third Unabridged (online). It is not
redistributable and has no public API, so the trainer ships an approximation:

| Layer | Source | Used for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Spellings | ENABLE word list (~173k) | is this an English word at all |
| Tags | SPECIALIST lexicon via `lemminflect` | part of speech, inflectional form, regular/irregular |
| Frequency | Norvig `count_1w` | difficulty tiers, answer-key ordering |
| Closed class | transcribed from the Handbook | pronouns, prepositions, conjunctions, interjections, linking verbs, auxiliaries, collective nouns |

Rules that follow from LT 22 and are enforced by the engine: lowercase a–z only (no
proper nouns, contractions, hyphens, apostrophes, diacritics), 4–10 letters.

A submitted word that is a real spelling but carries no tags is reported **UNVERIFIED** —
it is neither counted valid nor counted against accuracy, and the UI says why. That is
deliberate: wrongly rejecting a word a player legitimately found is worse for training
than declining to score it. The whole dictionary layer sits behind one interface
(`DictionaryProvider`) so a club with legitimate access can substitute a better list.

## Open questions

Two things the official documents genuinely do not settle. Neither is invented around —
both are configuration with a visible label.

1. **The letter faces of the 23 cubes are not published.** The Tournament Rules,
   Handbook, Order of Play sheets and the step-by-step guide all describe the *colours*
   (4 red, 4 black, 4 green, 4 pink, 4 yellow, 3 orange) and two facts about content —
   two red cubes must contain `U`, and some red cubes have `C` instead — but no document
   lists the six faces of any cube. The trainer therefore ships a **clearly-labelled
   approximate cube set** (`data/cube-sets.ts`, id `approximate-2026`) and a settings
   screen where a club can type in the faces from a physical set. Everything downstream
   reads the cube set from configuration; nothing hard-codes letters.
   *If you can read me the 23 cubes off your club's set, that file is a five-minute fix
   and every generated shake becomes exact.*

2. **Whether "singular form / plural form" for a Senior verb means subject agreement or
   the bare/`-s` form.** The Senior Order of Play lists "1. singular 2. plural" under
   VERB with the footnote "the player may choose to designate present or past"; the
   Handbook (IV.C.1-2) says a verb agrees with its subject in number and that "when such
   a determination cannot be made, a judge should accept the explanation offered by the
   solution writer". The trainer treats the **third-person singular `-s` form** as
   satisfying *singular* and the **bare form** as satisfying *plural*, which is the
   morphological reading, and marks the demand as accepting a judge's ruling in play.
